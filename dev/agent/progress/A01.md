    - Small enough? Away from steep terrain, yes by four orders of magnitude for both uses: the convection trigger works on level pressures and temperatures (an error of a few Pa is far below a 1 K or 1 hPa sensitivity), and omega-to-w scales with 1/p, so the relative w error equals the relative p error (1e-5). In steep terrain the error is up to 1% in p (so 1% in w) at the lowest levels; small for w, but its cause, the height offset, is not small (below).

- incidents:
  - ERA5-ARCO stores: the surface geopotential (`geopotential_at_surface`, from ARCO's single-level store) disagrees with the orography under the model-level geopotential in steep terrain. The lowest model level (about 10 m above ground) appears below the surface in 5.8% (EUR) and 6.4% (NAM) of land cells, and 21-25% of cells above 1,000 m; offsets p95 16-19 m and up to 111-136 m over land (block EUR-Jan and NAM-Jan, one time each). GLIDE's heights above ground are (z - z_s)/g, so its terrain-following grid is shifted by that offset, moving residence in and out of the 0-40 m layer over mountains. Likely cause: ARCO regrids its model-level and single-level fields by different routes (task B13's subject). FLEXPART on flex_extract fields would not have it. Caught by the A01 a/b check. met-bazarr's validator already warns of levels below the surface but treats them as expected (they are, on pressure levels; on model levels they indicate this inconsistency).
